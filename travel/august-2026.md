    <section>
      <p class="eyebrow">Where we were</p>
      <h2>Quebec, Maine, and a lot of time in nature</h2>
      <p>We had a family holiday in Quebec, and it was honestly such a beautiful experience. We spent lots of time on the water, paddleboarding, and riding bikes, and it made me realize how much I enjoy being outside and moving at an unhurried pace.</p>
      <p>One of the things that really stood out was the drive from Maryland to Portland, Maine. After talking it over, we both agreed that a drive like that can be a meaningful part of the trip, not just a way to get from one place to another.</p>
      <p>It also made us think more clearly about what kind of travel we actually enjoy. We often prefer smaller cities close to nature rather than bigger metropolitan areas.</p>
    </section>
    <section>
      <p class="eyebrow">What we did</p>
      <h2>We leaned into routines, movement, and time outside</h2>
      <p>I’m currently reading <em>Designing Your Life</em>, which in many ways parallels the creative reflection of <em>The Artist’s Way</em>. It has been a good prompt to think about routines, attention, and the kind of days we want to create.</p>
      <p>Over the month, we also had a short time in DC, and then a broader stretch in Quebec and Montreal, which was genuinely lovely. We got to travel, spend time outside, move our bodies, and make room for reflection.</p>
      <ul>
        <li>Paddleboarding and time on the water</li>
        <li>Regular bike rides</li>
        <li>Reading and reflecting on life design</li>
        <li>Truing up routines and thinking more intentionally about attention and focus</li>
      </ul>
    </section>
    <section>
      <p class="eyebrow">The things we learned</p>
      <h2>Intentional living is more important than simply going more places</h2>
      <p>One of the biggest lessons from this month is that we don’t always need to cram in more travel or longer drives to feel like we have had a full, rewarding experience. A slower pace can leave more room to notice what is around us and enjoy the people we are with.</p>
      <p>We also learned that we are much more energized by nature-forward experiences than by trying to do too much in dense city settings. Even though we enjoy what larger cities offer, time outside and a little breathing room often make a trip feel more restorative.</p>
      <blockquote>We’ve realized that the most sustainable way to travel is not necessarily the most ambitious one—it’s the one that keeps us feeling well, connected, and present.</blockquote>
    </section>
    <section>
      <h2>On finances and what’s next</h2>
      <p>Overall, August felt rich, restorative, and full of learning. It reminded me that travel is best when it supports the life we want to live, not when it disrupts it.</p>
    </section>
